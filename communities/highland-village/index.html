Homes and neighborhoods

The city incorporated in the early 1960s beside Lewisville Lake. Handbook census counts show the houses that followed: 516 residents in 1970, 3,246 in 1980, 7,027 in 1990, and 12,173 in 2000 (Handbook of Texas).

The shoreline is the reservoir whose dam the Corps of Engineers completed in 1955, after the older Lake Dallas dam was replaced, and which took the Lewisville name in the mid-1970s (Handbook of Texas).

Copperas Branch Park is a lakeshore park of about 75 acres, leased from the Corps, with a boat ramp and a swim beach (City of Highland Village). Doubletree Ranch Park, at 310 Highland Village Road, has a splash pad, trails, soccer fields, and a fishing pier (City of Highland Village).

Pilot Knoll Park holds the city's other ramp onto the lake. The city reopens both ramps after high water once the lake is at 524 feet (City of Highland Village). A utility project notes that Brooks Court and Pilot Knoll were cut off from the rest of Highland Village by the lake until FM 2499 was built (City of Highland Village).

Getting around and work

Interstate 35E and Farm Road 407 are the roads the handbook uses to locate the city (Handbook of Texas). FM 2499 is the later connection that tied the Pilot Knoll side to the rest of Highland Village after the lake had split them (City of Highland Village).

By 2004 the city was recruiting businesses in order to expand retail along those roads (Handbook of Texas).

The A-train stop that carries the city's name, Highland Village/Lewisville Lake Station, is listed by DCTA at 2998 N. Stemmons Freeway in Lewisville. From there the line continues to Old Town and Hebron, both also addressed in Lewisville, and then to the DART Green Line at Trinity Mills (DCTA).

The handbook places Highland Village about 10 miles southeast of Denton and 20 miles northwest of Dallas (Handbook of Texas). DCTA's GoZone vans cover this city and Lewisville in one zone, seven days a week (DCTA).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Highland Village is in Denton County. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

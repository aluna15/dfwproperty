Homes and neighborhoods

J. A. Lynch settled the site in 1877 and laid out the town in 1881. A stage line to Millsap ran in 1882, the railroad arrived in 1891, and the Crazy Well was dug in 1885. By 1920 the handbook counted 400 mineral wells. The same entry says population from the 1940s through the 1970s moved with the military post at Camp Wolters, later Fort Wolters, and that a Weatherford College extension is in town (Handbook of Texas).

The city's October 2024 request for a unified development code repeats the count of 400 wells in 1920. It says Camp Wolters opened in 1925 as a cavalry training site, later became Fort Wolters, the army's Primary Helicopter Training Center, and closed in 1973. The former post is now Wolters Industrial Park. The same request says the Crazy Water Hotel is open again, the Baker Hotel is being restored, and a parks and recreation master plan was approved in December 2023. It also names downtown, the former Fort Wolters site, the area around Mineral Wells Regional Airport, and the area around Palo Pinto General Hospital as the places the comprehensive plan marks for more building (City of Mineral Wells).

Lake Mineral Wells State Park, at 100 Park Road 71, has a 640-acre lake, 12.8 miles of park trails, a 20-mile trailway, and 20 equestrian campsites. The park page says there is no skiing, tubing, or jet skis on the lake. Mineral Wells ISD's site lists the district office at 906 Southwest 5th Avenue in Mineral Wells (Mineral Wells ISD).

Getting around and work

U.S. 180 and U.S. 281 meet in Mineral Wells, and the handbook also places the city on the old Weatherford, Mineral Wells and Northwestern rail line (Handbook of Texas). The 2024 development-code request says a Mineral Wells Regional Airport master plan was prepared to TxDOT and FAA requirements for later capital work and for development on airport property (City of Mineral Wells).

Fort Wolters closed in 1973 (City of Mineral Wells) and the city now describes that ground as Wolters Industrial Park. The same request treats the old post, the airport edge, and the hospital edge as the growth areas outside downtown. The handbook's later industrial list, written beside the 1990 population figure, names clay pipe, aircraft systems, plastics, electronic products, brick, feed, and clothing among local products.

A Weatherford College extension is in Mineral Wells, according to the handbook. Lake Mineral Wells State Park, with the 640-acre lake and the 20-mile trailway, sits at a Mineral Wells address and is the large public recreation tract on this side of town (Texas Parks and Wildlife).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Mineral Wells is in Palo Pinto County, with parts in Parker County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Palo Pinto County addresses, the Palo Pinto County appraisal district (contact details) sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Palo Pinto County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

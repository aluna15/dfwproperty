Homes and neighborhoods

The land was part of the Peters Colony. Crawford Trees arrived from Illinois in 1845 and bought several thousand acres south of Camp Dallas. In 1855 he donated land for the Little Bethel Male and Female School. The Chicago, Texas and Mexican Central Railway arrived in 1880 and built Duncan Switch. Postmaster Charles P. Nance renamed the settlement Duncanville in 1882. A fire in 1884 destroyed most of the commercial buildings (Handbook of Texas).

Residents incorporated a 225-acre city on August 2, 1947, so Dallas would not annex them. When the population reached 5,000 in 1962, voters adopted a home-rule charter with a council-manager government. The handbook's population table lists 3,774 people in 1960, 14,105 in 1970, and 35,008 in 1990 (Handbook of Texas).

Commerce had centered on Main Street. By the late 1980s it had also moved to shopping centers on the west side of town and along U.S. Highway 67 between Duncanville and Cedar Hill. The handbook lists 1,307 businesses in 2000 (Handbook of Texas).

Getting around and work

Interstate 20 and U.S. Highway 67 are the highways that place Duncanville. The handbook puts the city west of their intersection and describes the U.S. Highway 67 stretch toward Cedar Hill as a main shopping corridor after Main Street was no longer the only business center. The Chicago, Texas and Mexican Central Railway reached the area in 1880, and the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe acquired that line in 1883 (Handbook of Texas).

Duncanville ISD serves the entire city and also portions of Dallas, Cedar Hill, and DeSoto. The district says it is one of the largest employers in the area, with about 1,600 employees, including almost 800 classroom teachers, at 18 campuses. It locates itself in southwest Dallas County along Interstate 20 and Highway 67 (Duncanville ISD).

DART's October 2025 description of its service area names 13 cities: Addison, Carrollton, Cockrell Hill, Dallas, Farmers Branch, Garland, Glenn Heights, Highland Park, Irving, Richardson, Rowlett, Plano, and University Park. Duncanville is not on that list (DART).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Duncanville is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The town site is older than the city government. City history says the settlement, earlier called Athol, had taken the name Keller by 1882 after the Texas and Pacific Railroad arrived, and that Keller incorporated on November 16, 1955. The same history credits U.S. Highway 377, running from Fort Worth toward Denton, along with the railroad (City of Keller history).

Hidden Lakes is a named master-planned community inside the city. Its owners association says the addition is in Keller, that it contains 16 named villages, and that the houses were built from 1996 through 2020 (Hidden Lakes HOA). The association's recorded management certificate places the plats in the Tarrant County map records (Texas HOA management certificate).

Big Bear Creek is the spine of the park system. The city says the citywide parks and trails are anchored by that creek (City of Keller). The Parks at Town Center, at 1100 Bear Creek Parkway, covers 102 acres and includes two lakes and 1.1 miles of hike-and-bike trail (City of Keller parks).

Keller ISD says it serves the entire City of Keller (Keller ISD). Northwest ISD says it serves part of Keller, along with parts of several other cities (Northwest ISD).

Getting around and work

Day-to-day travel is on the two roads the city names as primary corridors: U.S. 377 / Main Street and FM 1709 / Keller Parkway (City of Keller). Keller is not among the cities TxDOT lists on the I-820 loop (TxDOT I-820). Trinity Metro's station list has no Keller platform on TEXRail or the Trinity Railway Express (Trinity Metro). Northwest ISD covers part of Keller (Northwest ISD) and lists its offices at 2001 Texan Drive in Justin (Northwest ISD communities).

The city's about page names two business districts, historic Old Town Keller and Keller Town Center, as its economic engines, and it operates The Keller Pointe recreation and aquatics center (City of Keller). Keller ISD's offices are at 350 Keller Parkway in Keller. The district says it employs more than 4,000 staff and serves more than 30,000 students across 51 square miles, which it calls the third-largest land area in Tarrant County (Keller ISD).

Keller homes by the numbers

The median home in Keller was built in 1999. About 10% of homes predate 1980 and 19% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $594,300, above the Texas median of $283,800.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Keller is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

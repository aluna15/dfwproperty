Homes and neighborhoods

Everman started as a railroad town. The International-Great Northern Railroad arrived in 1904, and the new community took the name of a railroad engineer, John W. Everman, according to the Handbook of Texas. In 1917 it was one of three sites picked for a flight training school, Barron Field.

Most of the growth came later. The same entry counts about 450 residents in the mid-1950s and more than 5,000 by the mid-1970s, a jump it links to the building of Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport. For how old the housing is today, see the Census table further down this page.

The ZIP code is shared. City Hall is at 212 N. Race St., Everman, TX 76140 (Texas Municipal League), while industrial parks on West Everman Parkway carry Fort Worth addresses in the same 76140 ZIP (Holt Lunsford). Before you rely on Everman city services or its tax rate, confirm on the appraisal district record that the house is actually inside Everman.

Getting around and work

Several industrial parks sit west of town along West Everman Parkway and I-35W, inside Fort Worth. Fort Worth South Business Park, at 201 and 273 West Everman Parkway in Fort Worth, has two buildings of 88,671 and 400,900 square feet with immediate access to I-35. Nearby, Everman Trade Center fronts I-35W about 3.5 miles south of I-20, with 457,745 square feet in four buildings.

For the wider region, I-35W runs north into Fort Worth and I-20 crosses east to west a few miles north of those industrial parks (Holt Lunsford), and Interstate 820 is close by (Wikipedia).

Public transit is limited. Trinity Metro says on its On-Demand zones page that its South Tarrant rideshare zone no longer includes Everman: the service began with grant money, and the city opted to discontinue it because of budget constraints. For most trips, plan on driving.

Everman homes by the numbers

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Everman is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

John Balch settled in this area around 1870 and found three springs, one of which did not go dry. That spring was cleaned and bricked. In 1900 the spot was still scattered farms and a cemetery. The city incorporated on June 13, 1953, under a mayor and council, in order to avoid annexation by Dallas. The new limits took in pieces of Rylie, Kleberg, Five Points, Zipp City, Jonesville, and Triangle (Handbook of Texas).

Texas Power and Light brought electricity in 1939, and gas and telephone service came after World War II. Population was 3,500 by 1956 and 13,050 by 1976. A post office opened in September 1964. The Handbook table lists 6,821 people in 1960, 10,464 in 1970, 17,406 in 1990, and 19,375 in 2000 (Handbook of Texas).

Dallas ISD serves the southwestern portion of the city, and Mesquite ISD serves the northeastern portion (Wikipedia). Dallas ISD's own description lists Balch Springs among the cities inside its boundary (Dallas ISD). Mesquite ISD lists Floyd, Gray, Hodges, and Mackey elementary schools and A.C. New Middle School at Balch Springs addresses (Mesquite ISD).

Getting around and work

Interstate 635, Interstate 20, and U.S. Highway 175 are the through routes, and the Handbook places the city ten miles southeast of Dallas. It credits Interstate 635 with making Balch Springs a larger residential community. By the late 1970s, 95 percent of residents commuted to work in Dallas or Garland (Handbook of Texas).

In 1970 the town had three manufacturers, two printers, and a foundry. By 1991 the manufacturer count was seventeen, including shops that made shipping pallets and machine parts. That year the town also had two banks, a weekly newspaper, and a library. A 1988 vote to merge with Mesquite was ruled invalid, and Balch Springs stayed a separate city (Handbook of Texas).

Because of the proximity to Dallas, land values began to rise in the early 1970s. In 1958 the town had a fire department with three trucks. The first local taxes were levied in 1965, and a vote to disincorporate failed in 1966 (Handbook of Texas).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Balch Springs is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

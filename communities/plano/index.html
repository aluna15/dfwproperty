Homes and neighborhoods

Plano was platted and incorporated in 1873 and remained a farm town well into the twentieth century. In 1908 it became a stop on the Texas Electric Railroad. The Handbook of Texas counts 17,872 residents in 1970 and 72,331 in 1980, after Dallas's growth pulled housing out along U.S. Highway 75. It points to the Farrel-Wilson Farmstead Museum, also called the Heritage Museum, on a former sheep ranch, as the reminder of that farm landscape (Handbook of Texas).

The older street grid is the historic downtown. DART's Downtown Plano station stands on the east side of Haggard Park, at 15th Street and Avenue J (DART). Farther east, Oak Point Park and Nature Preserve covers 800 acres from Parker Road to Chaparral Road and from Spring Creek Parkway to Los Rios Boulevard. Arbor Hills Nature Preserve, on the western border, covers 200 acres (City of Plano).

Greenbelts thread between those areas. The Cottonwood Creek Greenbelt follows the eastern edge and includes 8.5 miles of the 2001 Six Cities Trail Plan shared with Allen, Frisco, Garland, McKinney, and Richardson. The Chisholm Trail follows a creek through central Plano. The Preston Ridge Trail runs north and south in west Plano, and the Legacy Trail follows White Rock Creek (City of Plano).

A city financial profile says 72 percent of Plano's property-tax value is single-family residences and commercial property combined. Legacy West is one of the districts along the toll roads where that commercial land and newer housing sit together (City of Plano).

Getting around and work

DART light rail reaches Plano on the Red Line and, during peak periods, the Orange Line. Stations in the city are Downtown Plano, Parker Road, and 12th Street. Parker Road, at Park Boulevard and Archerwood Street near U.S. Highway 75, is the last stop on the North Central corridor (DART). The 12th Street station, at 995 12th Street, also connects with the Silver Line (DART). From Downtown Plano, bus route 236 runs toward the Addison Transit Center and route 247 toward the South Garland Transit Center (DART).

The city's financial profile says the principal employers account for 17.5 percent of city employment, or 28,096 jobs. Names on that list include JPMorgan Chase, Capital One Finance, Toyota Motor North America, Liberty Mutual, Ericsson, AT&T Foundry, PepsiCo, NTT Data, Frito-Lay, and FedEx Office. The same profile notes membership in Dallas Area Rapid Transit and access to four airports: international, regional, municipal, and private (City of Plano).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Plano is in Collin County, with parts in Denton County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Collin County addresses, the Collin County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Collin County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The original townsite was six blocks, platted in 1904 beside the railway depot (Handbook of Texas). Addison incorporated in 1953 so Dallas would not annex it, and Addison Airport was built in 1956 (Handbook of Texas).

Addison Circle is the later residential district, on the eastern edge of town. It began as a vacant 80-acre site between Addison Airport and the Dallas Tollway and later grew to 124 acres. Construction started in 1993, and the project was marked complete in 2016. Columbus Realty Trust began the buildings and Post Properties finished them. The project filled vacant land inside the town rather than rebuilding an older shopping center. There are more than 2,400 residences, counting brownstones, townhouses, condominiums, and apartments (Congress for the New Urbanism).

Parks the town lists among those blocks include Addison Circle Park, Les Lacs Park, Vitruvian Park, Beckert Park, and Quorum Park (Town of Addison).

Getting around and work

Belt Line Road and the Dallas North Tollway are the highways the Handbook of Texas uses to place Addison, 13 miles north of downtown, on the old Cotton Belt rail line. From 1903 to 1917 a small Cotton Belt train, the Plug, ran from Addison into Dallas (Handbook of Texas). The Silver Line uses that corridor: it is 26 miles long, stops at Addison Station, and also stops in Dallas at Knoll Trail Station and Cypress Waters Station before DFW Airport (DART). Downtown, the Red, Blue, Green, and Orange light-rail lines meet, on a system of 93 miles (DART).

Addison Circle includes about 625,000 square feet of office, retail, and restaurant space along with the housing (Congress for the New Urbanism). The Handbook of Texas describes Addison Airport, opened in 1956, and the aviation-equipment firms around it as the town's major industries in the years after the airport was built (Handbook of Texas).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Addison is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The name comes from Lafayette Brown, called Fate by his wife. A post office opened in July 1880. By the mid-1880s the settlement had a school, a cotton gin, two general stores, and 75 people. In 1886 the railroad arrived, Dr. Wylie T. Barnes platted Barnes City about a half mile northeast, and in February 1887 that new site kept the name Fate. By 1890 there were 15 businesses, a two-room school, and 100 residents. A bank opened in 1907, and the town incorporated in 1908. The count reached 299 by 1920, fell to 194 by 1930 and 127 by 1940, and was 497 in 2000 (Handbook of Texas).

A city planning appendix says three large planned developments, Chamberlain Crossing, Woodcreek, and Williamsburg, together cover nearly 30 percent of Fate's land. As of 2020 that appendix counted 64 acres of public parkland and 35 acres in private association parks, including six Woodcreek parks and two Williamsburg parks (City of Fate).

A 2020 city agreement for Williamsburg East covers about 164 acres at the northeast corner of FM 551 and Gettysburg Boulevard, about 585 single-family houses, about 16 acres to be dedicated as public park, and an amenity-center site of about 3 acres. Council adopted that planned development on January 6, 2020 (City of Fate). A later streets memo lists older subdivisions built before annexation without sidewalks: Melody Ranch, Northview Village, Sleepy Hollow, Southview Village, and Riding Club Estates. Most of those sit southeast of town off Blackland Road, near Joe Burger Park (City of Fate).

Getting around and work

State Highway 66 and the Missouri, Kansas and Texas line are the routes the Handbook uses to place Fate (Handbook of Texas). Council records discuss mixed-use projects at the northwest, northeast, southeast, and southwest corners of FM 551 and Interstate 30, plus a downtown central business area (City of Fate).

City Hall is at 1900 CD Boren Parkway. Drinking water comes from the North Texas Municipal Water District. Electric service is split between Farmers Electric Cooperative and the Oncor grid, and Atmos Energy supplies natural gas (City of Fate).

The city says STAR Transit serves Rockwall County and that the nearest DART rail stop is Downtown Rowlett Station, about 17 miles west. The same page says Rockwall ISD covers the west side of Fate and Royse City ISD the east side, and that Pioneer Technical Arts Academy is in Fate (City of Fate). Royse City ISD says portions of Fate are inside the district and that Kevin Worthy Fate High School, off FM 552, is planned to open in August 2027 under a May 2023 bond (Royse City ISD).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Fate is in Rockwall County. For Rockwall County addresses, the Rockwall County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Rockwall County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The site was occupied in 1852. In 1881 the settlement took the name Garza as the Dallas and Wichita Railway built through, and in 1926 or 1929, after the first lake was impounded, residents renamed it Lake Dallas (City of Lake Dallas).

That first dam, near Garza, was 80 feet high and 11,000 feet long, and work started in February 1928. The larger dam was completed in 1955. On October 28, 1957, the old dam was breached, and the combined reservoir was renamed Lewisville Lake in the mid-1970s (Handbook of Texas).

Lake Dallas incorporated in 1965 after a nine-year legal dispute. The city history page follows the population from 489 in 1940 to 1,431 in 1970 and 3,177 in 1980, and it lists 7,708 people in the 2020 census (City of Lake Dallas). City hall stands at 212 Main Street, in the older center (City of Lake Dallas).

Getting around and work

In 2009 the North Texas Tollway Authority finished a bridge 1.7 miles long over Lewisville Lake, connecting El Dorado Parkway on the Lake Dallas side with Swisher Road. A six-lane thoroughfare carries traffic between that bridge and Interstate 35E. A bridge from the 1920s had been removed in the 1950s when the lake was enlarged (City of Lake Dallas).

The handbook counted 113 businesses in Lake Dallas in 1991 (Handbook of Texas). The 1881 railroad is what fixed Garza on the map. The roads that carry work trips now are Interstate 35E and that 2009 bridge toward Little Elm (City of Lake Dallas). Lake Dallas ISD's central office is in the city at 104 Swisher Road (Lake Dallas ISD). The district's own description places it on the I-35 corridor about 30 miles from downtown Dallas and says the boundaries include all or part of Lake Dallas (Lake Dallas ISD).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Lake Dallas is in Denton County. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

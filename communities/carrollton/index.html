Carrollton sits on Interstate 35E where Dallas, Denton, and Collin counties meet, fourteen miles north of downtown Dallas (Handbook of Texas). Homes and neighborhoods

Carrollton incorporated in 1913. The Handbook of Texas says the name came from Carrollton, Illinois, and that almost three-quarters of the year-round housing units were built between 1970 and 1980, when the count of residents rose from 13,855 to 40,595 (Handbook of Texas).

The older center is the downtown square. DART places Downtown Carrollton Station at Belt Line Road and Broadway Street, 1013 N. Denton Drive, beside historic Downtown Carrollton Square, with a pedestrian bridge over Belt Line Road (DART).

North of the square, the city marks the former Josey Ranch at 1440 Keller Springs Road. It says C.W. Josey bought the first 70 acres in the 1940s and kept adding land until the ranch covered 1,000 acres. The lake on the site is a former clay quarry, and the city uses the remainder as a sports complex (City of Carrollton).

Offices and the master plan called Austin Ranch sit on a later tract. A city announcement said AmerisourceBergen leased 300,000 square feet at the Offices of Austin Ranch, inside a 1,700-acre master-planned development that already included Freddie Mac, Quest, Sanyo, Orthofix, and Edward Don (City of Carrollton).

Getting around and work

Interstate 35E is the north-south freeway (Handbook of Texas). The city's zoning notes treat Trinity Mills Road at Josey Lane as the President George Bush Turnpike (City of Carrollton). At the southeast corner of that turnpike and I-35E, the city and DART own nearly 26 acres around Trinity Mills Station (City of Carrollton).

When the Green Line opened, the city listed three Carrollton platforms: Downtown Carrollton at Belt Line Road and Broadway Street, Trinity Mills at Trinity Mills Road and Broadway Street, and North Carrollton/Frankford at West Frankford Road and Trade Center Drive (City of Carrollton). The DCTA A-train is a 21-mile line that reaches the Green Line at Trinity Mills after stops in Denton, Lewisville, and Hebron (DCTA).

DART started Silver Line service on October 25, 2025. The agency describes a route of more than 26 miles through Grapevine, Coppell, Dallas, Carrollton, Addison, Richardson, and Plano, ending at DFW Airport. Downtown Carrollton is a Silver Line station as well as a Green Line stop (DART).

Western Extrusions, in the Valwood Industrial Park, is a manufacturer the city called a major employer, with a base of about 850 jobs. The city and DART set up GoLink trips from Downtown Carrollton Station to the plant on Sandy Lake Road (City of Carrollton).

Carrollton homes by the numbers

The median home in Carrollton was built in 1988. About 26% of homes predate 1980 and 16% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $381,000, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Carrollton is in Denton County, with parts in Dallas County and Collin County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

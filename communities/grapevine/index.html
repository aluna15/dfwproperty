Homes and neighborhoods

Two big public projects shaped where Grapevine's homes went. The first was the lake: the Army Corps of Engineers began the dam on Denton Creek in January 1948 and finished it in June 1952, and Grapevine Lake is still owned by the federal government and run by the Corps' Fort Worth district for flood control and water supply (Handbook of Texas). The second was the airport, which opened in 1974 just southeast of town; Grapevine's population went from 7,023 in 1970 to 29,198 in 1990 (Handbook of Texas).

The housing that came out of those decades is mixed. The city's economic development office describes homes that run from ranch-style houses to brick colonials to large estates on bigger lots, alongside apartment complexes (Grapevine Economic Development).

The old center is Main Street. Liberty Park there holds the Torian Cabin, preserved by the Grapevine Historical Society (Grapevine Economic Development), and the TEXRail station sits on the same street.

Much of the city's outdoor space is tied to the lake and the park system. Recreation sites listed by the economic development office include:

  • The Vineyards Campground & Cabins, with lake-view cabins and RV sites.
  • Parr Park and its sprayground, and the Oak Grove Baseball Complex.
  • The REC of Grapevine, a multigenerational recreation center (Grapevine recreation).

Getting around and work

Highways meet at the airport's north entrance. TxDOT's DFW Connector rebuilt 8.4 miles of SH 114 and SH 121 in Grapevine, Southlake and Irving (TxDOT), and the city's economic development office also lists I-635 among the routes serving Grapevine (Grapevine Economic Development).

TEXRail trains stop at Grapevine/Main Street and DFW Airport North on the way to DFW Airport Terminal B, and run west through North Richland Hills to downtown Fort Worth (Trinity Metro). The city notes that the airport sits partly inside city limits (Grapevine Economic Development).

Major employers named by the city (Grapevine major employers):

  • DFW International Airport
  • Gaylord Texan Resort and Convention Center
  • Baylor's medical center in Grapevine
  • Bass Pro Shops Outdoor World
  • Kubota, which moved its North American headquarters to Grapevine

The city lists accommodations and food services among its top industries (Grapevine Economic Development). The Grapevine Convention Center opened in 1988 and Grapevine Mills in 1997 (Handbook of Texas).

School districts

Addresses in Grapevine fall into these public school districts:

  • Grapevine-Colleyville ISD
  • Carroll ISD

District lines do not follow city limits. Confirm the district for a specific address with the district itself before you write an offer.

Grapevine homes by the numbers

The median home in Grapevine was built in 1993. About 16% of homes predate 1980 and 18% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $493,100, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates (tables b08013, b08303, b25003, b25018, b25024, b25034, b25035, b25064, b25077).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Grapevine is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

Irving remained a small farm town well into the twentieth century. The Handbook of Texas population table lists 2,621 residents in 1950. Developers then bought the farmland for houses, and the same table lists 45,985 residents in 1960. The handbook names older settlements the city later took in, including Bear Creek, Union Bower, and Sowers (Handbook of Texas).

Las Colinas is the large planned district on the north side. In 1973 Ben H. Carpenter began converting his family's ranch, El Ranchito de las Colinas, under a master declaration (Las Colinas Association). A history the association published puts the land he had assembled at about 6,000 acres (Las Colinas history). The association now describes more than 40 neighborhoods, 190 acres of parkland, and 17 miles of walkways and canals (Las Colinas Association).

Valley Ranch is a separate planned community in the northernmost part of Irving, north of Interstate 635. The Irving Chamber describes 2,400 acres there, with 4,000 single-family houses, 22 multifamily properties, and landscaped canals (Irving Chamber).

Campion Trail is the city's long greenbelt on the Elm Fork and West Fork of the Trinity River. The master plan, approved in 1995, calls for 22 miles. Two pieces are open: 8.5 miles on the Elm Fork and 6.5 miles on the West Fork. A 5.5-mile link is planned between the Las Colinas segment and Trinity View Park, and the south end already meets Grand Prairie's Lone Star Trail (City of Irving).

Getting around and work

State Highway 161, the President George Bush Turnpike, ties State Highway 183 to State Highway 114 and Interstate 635, and continues south toward Interstate 30. State Highway 360 lies just west of Irving and is reached from State Highway 183 (Visit Irving). In the 1970s Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport was built along the city's western edge (Handbook of Texas).

DART counts six Orange Line stations in Irving: University of Dallas, Las Colinas Urban Center, Irving Convention Center, Hidden Ridge, Dallas College North Lake, and Belt Line. The convention center opened in early 2011, and the first piece of the Orange Line, including the convention center station, opened in August 2012. Hidden Ridge Station opened in April 2021 next to Verizon's Hidden Ridge development. The Trinity Railway Express stops at Downtown Irving/Heritage Crossing and at West Irving (DART).

Las Colinas Urban Center Station sits beside Lake Carolyn, with apartment buildings along the track and a promenade around the lake. A free fixed-route shuttle called The Loop circulates through the Urban Center (Visit Irving).

The city's economic development page, using Chamber figures, lists private employers that include Citi (6,678), Vistra Corp. (4,910), Allstate (3,843), Verizon (3,400), DFW International Airport (2,220), and Microsoft (2,075). The same page says more than 8,500 businesses operate in Irving (City of Irving).

District lines do not follow city limits. Confirm the district for a specific address with the district itself before you write an offer.

Irving homes by the numbers

The median home in Irving was built in 1986. About 36% of homes predate 1980 and 14% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $315,600, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Irving is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe finished its line from Cleburne to Fort Worth in 1881 and missed Caddo Grove, two miles west of the later town. A station went up on the tracks instead. The handbook says the plat was surveyed in 1880, the settlement was organized in 1881, and a post office opened in 1882. By 1890 it counted about 300 residents, two steam corn-mill gins, a hotel, a general store, and a newspaper, the Johnson County Record.

Those first houses were town lots around a rail stop, not a modern master-planned tract. State Highway 174 and Farm Road 917 are still the two roads that fix where the old grid sits. The land is in the Cross Timbers, on a survey (Handbook of Texas) patented by W. W. Byers in 1867 and sold to John Powell in 1874.

Before you write an offer, match the address to the city limit and to the school-district line. Joshua ISD's own page says the district covers 76 square miles and takes in the Joshua, Burleson, and Cleburne communities, so the district is much larger than the city (Joshua ISD).

Getting around and work

Farm produce was what the railroad hauled out of Joshua. The handbook calls that freight the town's main export in its early years. State Highway 174 runs southwest toward Cleburne and northeast toward Burleson. Farm Road 917 is the east-west road at the same crossing. Fires in 1900 and 1912 damaged the business row, and a public water system started in 1906.

In 1912 (Handbook of Texas) the Fort Worth South Traction Line opened between Cleburne and Fort Worth and stopped in Joshua. The handbook says that interurban ended in 1932. The first car bought in town was in 1913. By 1914 the same entry counted 824 residents, two cotton gins, an ice plant, and a bank.

The Santa Fe line is the historic freight railroad, not a passenger schedule you can read off a current city page. For a job outside town, the practical trip is by car on Highway 174 or Farm Road 917. The district office of Joshua ISD is at 310 E. 18th Street in Joshua, and the district dates its start to 1902 (general information).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Joshua is in Johnson County. For Johnson County addresses, the Johnson County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Johnson County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

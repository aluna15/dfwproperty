Homes and neighborhoods

The city’s historical overview traces the first settlement to September 1844, when Roderick Rawlins took a Republic of Texas headright, and says A. Bledsoe laid out the townsite in the spring of 1852 and named it for Lancaster, Kentucky. Lancaster incorporated in 1853, and voters incorporated again in 1882. That overview puts the housing stock at 75% single-family, 24% multifamily, and 1% other, across 12,610 residential parcels.

The parks division manages more than 600 acres. The overview describes a 170-acre Community Park with a 6-acre pond, a fishing pier, and the Royce Clayton ballpark, whose covered grandstand holds 500 people, plus a 60,000-square-foot recreation center and an 11,500-square-foot Senior Life Center beside the water. The parks page says the Helen Giddings Amphitheater in that park was finished in September 2013. Bear Creek Nature Park has equestrian and walking trails, two ponds, a fishing pier, and campsites. Smaller parks named in the overview include Meadow Creek, Dewberry, Rocky Crest, Verona, and Stanford. The historic square still centers on the Interurban Building, built in 1911 for the Texas Interurban Railway that ran through Lancaster between Waxahachie and Dallas.

Getting around and work

The historical overview draws the city as 33 square miles edged by Interstate 35E on the west, Interstate 20 and Dallas on the north, and Interstate 45 and Hutchins on the east. It also names Lancaster Regional Airport next to the freight rail and highway network. The community profile says Loop 9 is under construction as another route between Interstate 35E and Interstate 45, and that DART bus service reaches the Cedar Valley campus, with rail half a mile north of the city limits.

On the employers page, Walmart is listed at 450 workers, Brass Craft at 380, Oak Creek Homes at 280, Cedar Valley College at 225, and the city itself at 212. AT&T’s material distribution center, Swift Transportation, and United Natural Foods are listed at 200 each, and Consolidated Mail Pharmacy at 200 as well. The same list includes Brentwood Companies at 185, ONCOR at 135, Home Depot at 130, and Aquatic at 120.

Lancaster homes by the numbers

The median home in Lancaster was built in 1991. About 31% of homes predate 1980 and 11% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $246,300, below the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Lancaster is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

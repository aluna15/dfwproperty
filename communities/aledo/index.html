Homes and neighborhoods

The city's about page dates a Texas and Pacific Railroad station here to 1879, first called Parker Station, and says the community was renamed Aledo in 1882 for a railroad official's hometown in Illinois. It incorporated in 1963. The same page gives a 2025 population estimate of 5,521 and describes a Front Street district of shops and restaurants (City of Aledo). The handbook adds that the early settlement was also called Parker's Station, that an Aledo post office opened in 1882, and that the town had an estimated 400 residents by the early 1920s (Handbook of Texas).

By 1915 the handbook counted a steam cotton gin, a corn mill, a bank, and 21 businesses, and it called Aledo a retail stop for farms in eastern Parker County. Growth after the mid-1970s followed Fort Worth's outward push. The city address on the about page is 200 Old Annetta Road, and that page describes a municipal complex bringing city hall, the police department, and the public library onto one site.

The parks page describes playgrounds, open green space, walking trails, and a mountain-bike pump track, without listing acreage (Aledo parks and trails). Aledo ISD says the district covers 130 square miles in Parker and Tarrant counties and includes all or part of Aledo, plus Annetta, Annetta North, Annetta South, Cresson, Fort Worth, Hudson Oaks, and Willow Park. The city's business directory lists that district as the public prekindergarten-through-12 system, with its administration building at 1008 Bailey Ranch Road (City of Aledo directory).

Getting around and work

Farm Road 1187 is the highway the handbook uses to place Aledo, fifteen miles west of Fort Worth in southeastern Parker County (Handbook of Texas). The city's about page describes the same spot as just west of Fort Worth, with the Front Street district as the local shopping row (City of Aledo).

The railroad is what fixed the town's location. Tracks of the Texas and Pacific were laid nearby, businesses clustered by the rail line, and a Texas and Pacific official suggested the name Aledo. By the mid-1880s the handbook estimated 150 residents and called the stop a shipping point for area farms. The steam gin and corn mill were still part of that farm trade in 1915.

The district administration building at 1008 Bailey Ranch Road is in Aledo. The city directory lists Aledo High School at 1000 Bailey Ranch Road and Aledo Middle School at 416 FM 1187 South, plus a campus at 990 Bailey Ranch Road (City of Aledo directory). City hall remains listed at 200 Old Annetta Road.

Aledo homes by the numbers

Most of Aledo's housing is recent. The median home was built in 2010, and 52% of homes went up in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $465,100, above the Texas median of $283,800.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Aledo is in Parker County. For Parker County addresses, the Parker County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Parker County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

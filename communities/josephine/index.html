Homes for sale in Josephine, Texas

Josephine is east of the Dallas-Fort Worth area, with land in Collin County and in Hunt County. City history says the St. Louis, Arkansas and Texas Railway reached the site in 1888, and that by 2000 the town had expanded into Hunt County. A 2022 comprehensive-plan presentation says the city incorporated in 1955 (City of Josephine history, 2022 plan presentation).

Homes and neighborhoods

The residents page lists homeowner associations for Magnolia Pointe, Fountain View South, Fountain View North, Waverly Estates, and Riverfield. It then separates several municipal utility districts from the city limits. Magnolia Pointe phases 1–10 are listed at 1,918 lots, Waverly phases 1–4 at 784 lots, and Riverfield phases 1–4 at 821 lots. The page says those MUDs sit in the extraterritorial jurisdiction, that the post office may still allow a Josephine mailing address, and that Riverfield's district directory entry covers Hunt and Collin counties. Wildflower MUD is listed as 3,300 homes still to be developed, with no homes built yet, and Hunt County MUD No. 3 had a 2024 debt election and no homes built yet (City of Josephine residents page).

City Hall is at 201 S. Main Street. The city home page says Josephine is served by Community ISD (City of Josephine). Community ISD's contact list places Ellis Elementary at 900 N. Main Street in Josephine and Roderick Elementary at 1201 Cotton Gin Court in Josephine (Community ISD contacts). The district's 2024–25 zone note says a future Community Creek Middle School is planned in Josephine, with the opening date still to be set (Community ISD zones).

Community ISD says it was established in 1947, that its boundaries cover 89 square miles, and that those boundaries take in Copeville, Josephine, Lavon, and Nevada. The district home page counts more than 5,200 students and eight schools (Community ISD).

Getting around and work

City history says the 1888 townsite followed the Cotton Belt, and that a Cotton Belt passenger train ran through Josephine to Dallas in 1889. The 2022 plan presentation says that line, as it continues into Collin and Dallas counties, is now part of DART, and that the thoroughfare plan was drawn with the Collin County and Hunt County transportation plans. The presentation also says TxDOT was then planning FM 6 as four lanes, with an ultimate six-lane section (City of Josephine history, 2022 plan presentation).

A TxDOT notice posted by the city describes reconstruction of FM 6 from State Highway 78 to east of FM 1777, about 7.9 miles, passing through Lavon, Nevada, and Josephine. The existing two-lane road would become a four-lane divided highway, with an ultimate six-lane section and a raised median, plus a continuous 10-foot shared-use path on each side. Existing right-of-way is 60 to 100 feet; the proposed width is 140 to 220 feet. The notice says the project could need about 53 acres of additional right-of-way and could displace 20 residences and one non-residential structure (TxDOT FM 6 notice).

The city's thoroughfare map labels Highway 78 at Lavon Lake and shows FM 6 crossing the Hunt County line (Josephine thoroughfare plan). Community ISD's offices are in Nevada, at 611 North FM 1138, while Ellis and Roderick elementaries are the district campuses with Josephine street addresses (Community ISD contacts).

Josephine homes by the numbers

Most of Josephine's housing is recent. The median home was built in 2013, and 63% of homes went up in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $320,200, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Josephine is in Collin County, with parts in Hunt County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Collin County addresses, the Collin County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Collin County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

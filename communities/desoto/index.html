Homes and neighborhoods

A store built in 1848 at the junction of what became Belt Line and Hampton roads was the start of the settlement, and DeSoto incorporated in 1949, according to the Handbook of Texas. Later suburban building left a housing pattern the city’s 2035 strategic plan still calls mostly single-family. Citing a 2025 Lightcast overview, that plan splits structures into 77% single-unit homes, 21% multi-unit buildings, and 2% mobile homes, on 21.67 square miles of land, and it estimates that 80% of the city was already built out as of 2025.

Parks sit inside those blocks rather than on one edge of town. The city parks page lists Ernie Roberts Park, Moseley Park, and Les Zeiger Park as community parks, and Briarwood, Kiva, Murphy Hills, North Elerson, Townsend, and Mirkes as neighborhood parks. Regional places on the same list are the DeSoto BMX Track, Curtistene S. McCowan Park, and Grimes Park. The trail names are the DeSoto Ranch Nature Trail, the Roy Orr Trail, and the Paul S. Dryer Preserve at Windmill Hill. The strategic plan also points to the McCowan Aquatic and Recreation Center as a newer recreation building among older streets.

The FY 2026-27 budget says property inside the city lies in three school districts, and that only a very limited area is in Duncanville ISD.

Getting around and work

DeSoto has 4 miles of frontage on the west side of Interstate 35E, sits less than 1 mile south of Interstate 20, and is 1 mile east of U.S. Highway 67. Farm-to-Market 1382, Belt Line Road, runs east and west and connects U.S. 67 to Interstate 35E, the city budget says. A city ordinance names two truck routes: Interstate 35E between the north and south limits, and Belt Line Road between the west and east limits, according to the transportation plan.

That same plan says DeSoto is not served by any DART transit line, and that the only public transportation inside the city limits is a STAR Transit shuttle for qualifying riders. It describes Loop 9 as a road that would pass just south of DeSoto, from U.S. Highway 67 near Cedar Hill to Interstate 20 near Seagoville (transportation plan).

Workplaces cluster on Interstate 35E. The budget, citing the DeSoto EDC as of October 2023, lists DeSoto ISD at 1,104 employees, Kohl's e-commerce at 800, the city at 468, Solar Turbines at 350, Williamsburg Village at 350, GlasFloss Industries at 300, and Marten Transport, Walmart Distribution, and Hickory Trail Hospital at 250 each. More than 400 acres in the DeSoto Eagle Industrial and Business Park are marked for industrial and commercial sites, including Hillwood's Crossroads Trade Center, with 1.2 million square feet at Centre Park Boulevard and Interstate 35E (FY 2026-27 budget).

DeSoto homes by the numbers

The median home in DeSoto was built in 1993. About 21% of homes predate 1980 and 14% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $319,400, above the Texas median of $283,800.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

DeSoto is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

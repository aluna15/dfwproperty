Homes and neighborhoods

The city’s 2004 comprehensive plan says voters incorporated a village in 1941 and the board renamed it the City of River Oaks on May 7, 1946. The plan puts the city at about 1.9 square miles on the West Fork of the Trinity, just downstream from Lake Worth, with no extra land left to annex. A field survey in October 2003 found the city about 99 percent built, with single-family houses on 937 acres, about three-quarters of the developed land, and only about 1 percent vacant.

That survey groups the houses into five areas split by Roberts Cut-Off, Meandering Road, and River Oaks Boulevard, which is State Highway 183. Apartment land was about 5 acres. The city-owned park is McGee Park, with ball fields and a playground; the first playground opened on September 6, 1947. The eastern part of YMCA Camp Carter, east of the West Fork, is inside River Oaks and includes the equestrian center. Castleberry ISD includes the whole city of River Oaks.

Getting around and work

River Oaks Boulevard is State Highway 183, a six-lane divided road that TxDOT maintains. Jacksboro Highway, State Highway 199, clips the city for one block between River Oaks Boulevard and Long Avenue. Meandering Road is the four-lane arterial, and Roberts Cut-Off is the two-lane arterial. The plan says many daily trips pass through rather than start in town.

The same plan says Naval Air Station Joint Reserve Base Fort Worth, formerly Carswell Air Force Base, sits next to River Oaks. Meandering Road and Sam Calloway Road are the local roads toward the east gate. The plan names Lockheed Martin, beside the base, as a main employment center for the area. The base history places the installation inside Fort Worth, seven miles north-northwest of downtown, with the aircraft plant dating to 1941.

TEXRail’s station list does not include River Oaks. That commuter train runs between downtown Fort Worth and DFW Airport.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

River Oaks is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

South of downtown, the Cedars was the first residential subdivision, built in the 1880s (Handbook of Texas). Oak Cliff was a separate city until Dallas annexed it in 1903 (Dallas Municipal Archives). Winnetka Heights, south of the Trinity in Oak Cliff, was developed in 1908 about two miles from downtown (Preservation Dallas).

Dallas annexed East Dallas on January 1, 1890 (City of Dallas archives). A 1905 subdivision north of Old East Dallas ran along Swiss Avenue, where houses had to be two stories with a 60-foot setback (Preservation Dallas). Landmark neighborhoods also include Munger Place, South Boulevard-Park Row, and State-Thomas (Handbook of Texas). Pleasant Grove, about eight miles southeast near Lake June Road and Buckner Boulevard, gained houses after World War II; a shopping center opened in 1952, and the area was inside the city by 1962 (Handbook of Texas).

Later building ran mostly north and east (Handbook of Texas). Highland Park and University Park are separate cities in that belt, with Dallas around them (Handbook of Texas). Coppell ISD includes a Dallas area called Cypress Waters (Coppell ISD) and describes it as multi-family housing with no single-family houses (Coppell ISD).

Some 406 parks cover 50,000 acres. White Rock Lake, Bachman Lake, and Lake Cliff are surrounded by parks. The city bought White Rock Park in 1909 and made the lake the next year (City of Dallas archives). Klyde Warren Park is a deck over Woodall Rodgers Freeway between the Arts District and Uptown. Deep Ellum, on downtown's east edge, keeps 1920s storefronts (Handbook of Texas).

Getting around and work

DART light rail covers 93 miles on four lines — Red, Blue, Green, and Orange — and all four pass through downtown Dallas. The stations serve South Dallas, South Oak Cliff, West Oak Cliff, the North Central Expressway corridor toward North Dallas, Richardson, and Plano, a northeast route to Garland and Rowlett, a northwest route to Farmers Branch and Carrollton, a southeast route to Pleasant Grove, and a route to Irving and DFW Airport (DART). The Trinity Railway Express joins downtown Dallas and downtown Fort Worth. The Silver Line is a separate 26-mile regional rail line through Dallas, with Knoll Trail Station and Cypress Waters Station, between Plano and DFW Airport (DART Silver Line).

The city lists Love Field among its municipal facilities, and Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport, which opened in 1974, drew corporate headquarters into Dallas (Handbook of Texas).

The City of Dallas Office of Economic Development names these firms among leading employers in the city (Office of Economic Development):

  • UT Southwestern Medical Center
  • Southwest Airlines
  • Parkland Health & Hospital System
  • AT&T
  • Texas Instruments
  • Methodist Dallas Medical Center

Dallas homes by the numbers

The median home in Dallas was built in 1981. About 48% of homes predate 1980 and 14% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $320,700, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Dallas is in Dallas County, with parts in Collin County and Denton County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The Texas Trunk laid tracks through here in 1880. C. F. Crandall gave the railroad a right-of-way across his land, and the community took his name. It became a shipping point for nearby farms. A post office opened in 1881. By 1884 the town had a gristmill, a cotton gin, a school, and a population of 50. The estimate was 150 by 1890 and 251 by 1904. By the mid-1920s the Handbook estimated 750 residents and 50 businesses, including two banks. The 1943 count was 500. In 1988 the count was 1,207 residents and 15 businesses. The 1990 count was 1,652, and the 2000 count was 2,774 (Handbook of Texas).

Crandall ISD says a deed filed on February 28, 1901, records that twenty residents of the village of Crandall asked for an election to incorporate for school purposes. That election was held on March 9, 1901. The school building was finished in 1903, with five teachers and an enrollment of about 225. The district says it is located in Crandall, in the southwest part of Kaufman County (Crandall ISD).

Getting around and work

U.S. Highway 175 and Farm Roads 148 and 3039 are the roads the Handbook uses to place Crandall, nine miles west of Kaufman. The Texas Trunk of 1880 is what made the spot a farm shipping point (Handbook of Texas).

Crandall ISD says nine campuses serve seven communities across 84 square miles, with about 7,500 students and about 935 employees. The administration building is at 400 West Lewis Street. That page also lists career courses in agricultural science, HVAC, culinary arts, information technology, and health science, and it gives the office phone as 972-427-6000. The about page lists Crandall High School, Central Middle School, Heartland Middle School, and the elementary campuses Dietz, Martin, Noble-Reed, Smith, Walker, and Wilson (Crandall ISD). By the mid-1920s the town supported 50 businesses, including two banks; in 1988 the Handbook counted 15 businesses (Handbook of Texas).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Crandall is in Kaufman County. For Kaufman County addresses, the Kaufman County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Kaufman County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

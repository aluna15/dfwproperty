Homes and neighborhoods

John M. Stephen settled here in 1854 and donated the land George B. Erath laid out when the county was organized in 1856. Stephen gave another 50 acres of timber and became the first postmaster in 1857. The town incorporated permanently in 1889, the year the Fort Worth and Rio Grande Railway arrived. Most of the stone buildings on the square date from the 1890s, the same decade John Tarleton Agricultural College opened (Handbook of Texas).

The city's education page says Stephenville ISD has about 3,600 students and covers more than 232 square miles. It lists Central Elementary, Chamberlin Elementary, Hook Elementary, Gilbert Intermediate, Henderson Junior High, and Stephenville High School (City of Stephenville). The staff directory places Bill Johnson Park and Optimist-Jaycee Park under Parks and Leisure (city staff directory).

Tarleton State University's about page dates the institution to 1899 and its place in the Texas A&M University System to 1917. It says the main campus is in Stephenville, at 1333 W. Washington, and reports 21,000-plus students and 137 programs of study (Tarleton State University). The handbook's 1983 description called Tarleton the town's largest employer (Handbook of Texas).

Getting around and work

U.S. 67, U.S. 377, and U.S. 281 meet on the North Bosque. The handbook puts that junction about 100 miles southwest of Dallas (Handbook of Texas). The Fort Worth and Rio Grande arrived in 1889. Coal mining mattered here after 1886 for about thirty years, and an oil boom from 1918 to 1920 faded because the important fields were outside Erath County. In 1953 an industrial foundation formed, and within ten years the handbook listed a creamery, hatcheries, feed mills, and meat-packing plants.

Tarleton's main campus remains in Stephenville. The university reports more than 21,000 students enrolled and 137 programs, and it gives the campus address as 1333 W. Washington (Tarleton State University). The city's education page names two research centers there, the Texas Institute for Applied Environmental Research and the Southwest Regional Dairy Center (City of Stephenville).

Ranger College's Erath County center opened in January 2010 at 1835 W. Lingleville Road. The building was renovated in 2014 so general education, continuing education, a cosmetology program, and a licensed vocational nursing program could share one site (City of Stephenville). Stephenville ISD, on the same page, is the public district based in the city, with about 3,600 students across more than 232 square miles.

Stephenville homes by the numbers

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Stephenville is in Erath County. For Erath County addresses, the Erath County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Erath County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

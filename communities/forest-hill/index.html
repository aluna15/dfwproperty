Homes and neighborhoods

The Handbook of Texas calls Forest Hill a residential suburb of Fort Worth by 1896. In 1911 it was a stop on the Fort Worth Southern Traction Company’s electric interurban between Fort Worth and Cleburne. The Handbook counts 25 residents and two businesses in 1925, about 90 residents in the late 1940s, and 1,519 by the mid-1950s, by which time the town had incorporated. Later counts in that entry are 3,800 in 1967, 10,250 in 1976, and 12,709 in 2015.

City Hall is at 3219 California Parkway, Forest Hill, TX 76119. The city lists Forest Hill Memorial Park and a civic and convention center among local public places. The same page says two public school districts cover the city, Everman ISD and Fort Worth ISD, and that the address decides which one applies. How old the houses are today is in the Census table on this page.

Getting around and work

The Handbook places Forest Hill at the crossing of Interstate 20 and Interstate 820, eight miles southeast of Fort Worth, and says later growth followed from being next to Fort Worth. The 1911 interurban to Cleburne is gone. Trinity Metro’s TEXRail stations do not include Forest Hill. The line runs from Fort Worth to DFW Airport.

City Hall at 3219 California Parkway is open weekdays from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The city bills water, sewer, and garbage through its own utility office and contracts trash and bulk pickup to Waste Connections. It says it does not offer recycling. The public-works number for sewer trouble during business hours is 817-531-5700. The same residents page lists a public library at 817-708-2051, a civic and convention center at 817-551-5354, and Memorial Park at 817-568-3009. City Hall’s main line is 817-568-3000.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Forest Hill is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

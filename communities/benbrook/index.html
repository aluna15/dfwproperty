Homes and neighborhoods

The Handbook of Texas says settlers called the place Miranda about 1857 and renamed it for James M. Benbrook after the Texas and Pacific Railway came through in 1876. A post office opened in 1880. The same entry links later growth to Benbrook Reservoir, completed in the early 1950s, and counts 100 residents in the late 1940s, 617 a decade later, 3,300 in 1965, and 9,900 by 1976.

The city’s 2020 comprehensive plan treats Benbrook Lake and the creeks around it as the land constraint. Dutch Branch Park, 278 acres at 1801 Winscott Road, is leased from the Corps of Engineers on the lake shoreline and holds ball fields, tennis courts, and a playground. The plan also lists Timber Creek Park, Mont Del Park, Twilight Park, Rolling Hills Park, and Team Ranch Park, kept as open space near Benbrook Boulevard, I-20, and the railroad. A plan photo shows Whitestone Ranch Phase II in October 2018.

A 2024 city agreement with Fort Worth ISD names campuses in Benbrook: Benbrook Elementary, Westpark Elementary, Benbrook Middle-High, and Western Hills High. The same agreement describes Benbrook Lakeside, bounded by Winscott Road, Benbrook Boulevard, and I-20, and Westpark, between Benbrook Boulevard, RM 2871, the city limits, and I-20. Getting around and work

U.S. 377, also called Benbrook Boulevard in the city plan, crosses Interstate 20 through town. The plan counts about 108 miles of city roads. City Hall, at 911 Winscott Road, is a 10,480-square-foot building constructed in 1976.

In November 2002 the city created a tax-increment zone of 1,148 acres along both sides of I-20, between the Clear Fork of the Trinity River and the BNSF Railway. That zone includes the Brookside residential area and retail on the west side of Benbrook Boulevard. A later note in the same record says a sewer line was extended for commercial parcels east of Benbrook Boulevard between I-20 and Old Benbrook Road, including 12.6 acres held by the Benbrook Economic Development Corporation.

The Corps of Engineers says Benbrook Lake provides flood-risk management for the Fort Worth area and stores water for the City of Fort Worth. Its lake office is at 7001 Lakeside Drive in Fort Worth. Trinity Metro’s TEXRail list does not include a Benbrook address.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Benbrook is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The history page says a post office opened on June 16, 1879, after I. T. Johnson and Henry King built a gristmill and a store opened nearby. By the early 1880s the settlement had 100 residents and at least a dozen businesses. The page says the town had incorporated by the early 1940s, when the population was listed as 390, and that the 2010 census counted 1,338 people (city history).

The city homepage displays a population figure of 5,500 and a median age of 48.3. City Hall is at 2313 Main Street (City of Caddo Mills).

A council resolution describes a proposed reinvestment zone of about 4,393.076 acres, generally south of State Highway 66 and north of Interstate 30, with some parcels directly south of Interstate 30, east of County Road 2648 and west of FM 36, inside the city limits and the extraterritorial jurisdiction (reinvestment zone resolution). On August 4, 2026, the council minutes record a final plat for Preserve at Brushy Creek Phase I, south of State Highway 66 and east of County Road 2617: about 75.411 acres in the Shelby County School Land Survey, Abstract No. 946, with 158 residential lots and 5 common-area tracts (August 4, 2026 council minutes).

Getting around and work

In 1886 the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railway laid tracks through town. The history page says that by 1892 the population was 500 and the number of businesses had doubled, and that the State National Bank opened in 1905 (city history). The city airport page places Caddo Mills Municipal Airport at 4246 FM 1565 (municipal airport).

A council agenda records an ordinance to sell city-owned land to the Texas Department of Transportation for a proposed Interstate 30 expansion (council agenda). The August 4, 2026 minutes also list a resolution for a TxDOT agreement on improvements at the municipal airport, plus a hearing on annexing about 21.04 acres of TxDOT right-of-way along State Highway 66, about 1.4 acres of county right-of-way on County Road 2134, and 3.3 acres along County Road 2506 and County Road 2508 (August 4, 2026 council minutes). The city homepage links to Caddo Mills ISD, and the district office is at 100 Fox Lane (City of Caddo Mills, Caddo Mills ISD).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Caddo Mills is in Hunt County. For Hunt County addresses, the Hunt County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Hunt County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

The Dallas and Wichita Railway named Corinth and laid tracks through the townsite in 1880. The community marked a 100th anniversary in 1980 (Handbook of Texas).

Houses multiplied with development along Interstate 35 in the 1970s. Handbook counts list 461 residents in 1970, 1,264 in 1980, 3,944 in 1990, and 11,325 in 2000. Into the 1980s a few independent farms were still operating in town (Handbook of Texas).

The parks division maintains over 150 acres, including 11 neighborhood parks, Corinth Community Park, the bark park Corinth Unleashed, and the Commons at Agora (City of Corinth). Corinth Community Park, at 3700 Corinth Parkway, has soccer and football fields, a pavilion, and restrooms (City of Corinth). City hall is at 3300 Corinth Parkway, the address printed on those park pages.

A 2021 ordinance, Planned Development 58, sets Landmark at Tower Ridge up for townhouses and apartments and extends Tower Ridge Drive from FM 2181 (City of Corinth).

Getting around and work

Interstate 35 is the road the handbook ties to Corinth's 1970s growth, when most workers commuted toward Lewisville, Denton, or Dallas (Handbook of Texas). FM 2181, labeled Teasley Lane on a city economic-development listing, lies between Interstate 35E to the east and FM 2499 to the west (City of Corinth). The Tower Ridge ordinance also calls FM 2181 Swisher Road (City of Corinth).

North Central Texas College's Corinth campus is at 1500 N. Corinth Street (City of Corinth). The parks and recreation office is at 3101 S. Garrison Road (City of Corinth). DCTA's station list has no stop inside Corinth; the A-train stations it names are in Denton, Lewisville, and Carrollton (DCTA).

Two districts place campuses inside the city limits. Denton ISD lists Hawk Elementary at 2300 Oakmont Drive and Crownover Middle School at 1901 Creekside Drive (Denton ISD). Lake Dallas High School is at 3016 Parkridge Drive, and Lake Dallas ISD's own description says the district includes part of Corinth (Lake Dallas ISD).

Corinth homes by the numbers

The median home in Corinth was built in 1997. About 6% of homes predate 1980 and 12% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $375,600, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates (tables b08013, b08303, b25003, b25018, b25024, b25034, b25035, b25064, b25077).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Corinth is in Denton County. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

James E. Patton settled on Red Oak Creek in 1844, near present-day Ovilla. A post office reached the area in 1847, and the settlement took the creek's name after Ellis County was formed in 1849. The handbook says wheat was the main crop before the Civil War, and that by 1988 farming was no longer the center of the local economy.

The Missouri, Kansas and Texas arrived in 1884 on an alignment about a mile northwest of the older houses. The line was finished in 1890 between Dallas and Waco, and the town then centered along the tracks. Fires in 1909 and 1919 burned large parts of that commercial row, so the oldest blocks are not a continuous 19th-century street (Handbook of Texas).

Newer houses follow Interstate 35 through northern Ellis County. The city's economic-development office is at 101 S. Live Oak Street, which is a useful civic address when you are checking whether a listing sits inside Red Oak.

Getting around and work

Interstate 35 is the highway the handbook uses to place Red Oak, about twenty miles south of Dallas. The Missouri, Kansas and Texas reached the area in 1884 on a line about a mile northwest of the older houses, and the tracks finished in 1890 are the Dallas-to-Waco freight connection that pulled the shops off the creek settlement.

The city's economic development page says Texas State Technical College's North Texas campus in Red Oak teaches electrical power and controls, logistics, diesel equipment, machining, drafting, networking, welding, and HVAC. The desk at 101 S. Live Oak Street is open weekdays from 8:00 a.m. until 5:00 p.m. That page lists Lee McCleary at 469-218-1208 and John Knight at 469-218-7787 as the economic development contacts. It also names Life School Red Oak, a public charter, as a campus in town separate from the ISD boundaries.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Red Oak is in Ellis County. For Ellis County addresses, the Ellis County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Ellis County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

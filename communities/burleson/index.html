Homes and neighborhoods

Shannon Creek and Village Creek both cross the city, and the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railroad runs through it, with the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe just to the west (Handbook of Texas). The town began in 1881, when the MKT planned a line from Fort Worth to Hillsboro and set a depot on the townsite. Grenville M. Dodge bought the townsite, and Henry C. Renfro named the stop Burleson.

The Old Town reinvestment zone began in 2005 and covers 720 acres along Interstate 35, including Old Town, east toward Spinks Airport and west to the State Highway 174 corridor.

A 2019 update of the Old Town plan measured the district at about 242 acres. Single-family houses occupied about 80 acres, roughly 35 percent of that land.

Bailey Lake Park, at 280 W. Hidden Creek Parkway, totals 49 acres and includes an 8-acre lake plus an 18-hole disc golf course. The path around the lake is 3,248 feet (0.6 miles). From the Hidden Creek Parkway bridge to Tinker Trail is another 4,336 feet (0.8 miles). Anglers age 17 and older need a Texas fishing license.

The parks division's current description lists Shannon Creek Park, and the first phase of another community park, as work headed toward construction.

Getting around and work

McLane Classic Foods cut the ribbon on a headquarters and processing plant at HighPoint Business Park East on July 23, 2019, after leaving Fort Worth. The building is 94,000 square feet, and the move brought 40 jobs.

The city announced that SPC Group, the company behind Paris Baguette, would put a 267,000-square-foot production plant on Vantage Drive in HighPoint Business Park, with 450 jobs named in that notice. Construction has started, with completion expected by 2027, so the 450 jobs are a projection (Milling MEA).

Interstate 35W, State Highway 174 and Farm Road 731 all run through Burleson (Handbook of Texas).

Ellison on the Plaza, at 135 W. Ellison Street, is a retail and office development beside Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza. The city paired that project with a public parking lot at 140 W. Bufford.

Burleson homes by the numbers

The median home in Burleson was built in 2002. About 19% of homes predate 1980 and 29% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $308,200, close to the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Burleson is in Johnson County, with parts in Tarrant County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Johnson County addresses, the Johnson County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Johnson County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

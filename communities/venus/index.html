Homes and neighborhoods

J. C. Smyth bought eighty acres in the late 1880s and cut town lots out of an abandoned cornfield. By the mid-1890s the handbook counted thirty-one houses and thirteen businesses. A post office branch opened in 1888, when the place had about ten residents, and voters incorporated the town in 1903.

Later counts in that Handbook entry are a population past 800 by the late 1920s, about 300 residents and nearly twenty businesses by the late 1940s, 977 people in 1990, and 910 in 2000. The 1990 note is the one that records houses on the Ellis County side of the line, not only in Johnson County.

City Hall is at 700 W. U.S. Highway 67. It posts a four-day week: open 7:30 a.m.–5:30 p.m., Monday–Thursday. Use that highway address when you are checking whether a listing is inside the city.

Getting around and work

By 1890 Venus sat where the International-Great Northern met the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe, according to the handbook. Those were freight railroads. In the following decade that entry says three banks opened and a weekly paper, the Venus Express, started. The road on the city's current letterhead is U.S. Highway 67, the same corridor the handbook calls State Highway 67, about twenty miles east of Cleburne.

The phone listed for City Hall is 972-366-3348, and that page lists the counter on a four-day week, 7:30 a.m. until 5:30 p.m., with Friday closed. Venus ISD's site says the district, founded in 1914, is at 100 Student Drive and serves more than 2,300 students (Venus ISD). There is no separate employer table on the city homepage, so the highway, the old rail junction, and the district office are the workplaces the public pages actually locate in town.

Venus homes by the numbers

Most of Venus's housing is recent. The median home was built in 2014, and 66% of homes went up in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $270,500, close to the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Venus is in Johnson County, with parts in Ellis County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Johnson County addresses, the Johnson County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Johnson County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

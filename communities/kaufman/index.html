Homes and neighborhoods

Dr. William P. King arrived in 1840, bought 2.5 square miles, and built a fort on a hill. The outpost was called Kings Fort, the nearby stream became King's Creek, and the community was Kingsboro. Kaufman County was created in 1848 and named for David S. Kaufman. In 1852 Kingsboro became the county seat and took the county's name. The town incorporated in 1872 and again in 1881, the year the Texas Trunk Railroad arrived. The Texas Central connected Kaufman with Dallas in 1883. By 1884 the estimate was 1,000 residents and 40 businesses. In 1900 the estimate was 2,300 residents and 60 businesses. A tornado in 1963 damaged the business area. In 1990 the Handbook counted 5,238 residents and 108 businesses, including a dozen plants making steel products, furniture, and clothing. In 2000 it counted 6,490 residents and 416 businesses (Handbook of Texas).

Kaufman ISD says the city itself has a population of approximately 7,000, and that smaller communities including Ola, Rand, Jiba, and Oak Grove also fall inside the district. The district counts seven campuses and about 4,300 students: Helen Edwards Early Childhood Center, J. W. Monday Elementary, J. R. Phillips Elementary, Lucille Nash Elementary, O. P. Norman Junior High, Kaufman High School, and Gary W. Campbell High School (Kaufman ISD).

Getting around and work

U.S. Highway 175, State Highway 34, and State Highway 243 meet in Kaufman, 34 miles east of Dallas (Handbook of Texas). Kaufman ISD describes the same Highway 175 approach as 31 miles east of downtown Dallas (Kaufman ISD).

The Texas Trunk reached the city in 1881, and the Texas Central tied it to Dallas in 1883. Those lines made Kaufman the shipping point for county farms. By 1945 the Handbook counted 80 businesses, including a uniform factory, serving 2,654 residents. The 1990 list of local plants covered steel products, furniture, and clothing (Handbook of Texas). The district office remains at 1000 South Houston Street (Kaufman ISD). The first bank opened in the late 1880s, a second bank opened in 1892, an opera house was open by 1884, and three newspapers were publishing by 1900. The 1936 count was 2,279 residents (Handbook of Texas).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Kaufman is in Kaufman County. For Kaufman County addresses, the Kaufman County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Kaufman County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

Arlington's single-family housing is largely established. A July 2024 city single-family housing profile based on Tarrant Appraisal District records counted 99,367 single-family homes, put their median year built at 1984 and found that more than 65% went up before 1990. The report places the older homes near Downtown and in East Arlington, and the newer ones in Viridian and parts of south Arlington.

Viridian is a master-planned community on land along the Trinity River west of SH 360 and north of I-30 (CultureMap). Its developer, Johnson Development, describes a 2,000-acre plan for about 3,500 homes, with 600 acres of nature reserve, 500 acres of lakes, streams and wetlands, and a lake club with four pools.

Viridian sits next to River Legacy Park, a 1,031-acre city park of floodplain forest along the Trinity in northwest Arlington with more than 10 miles of off-road bike trails. The Trinity and its tributary Johnson Creek both run through the city (Wikipedia), so look at the flood-zone map for any house near those waterways before you write an offer.

Getting around and work

Arlington's main highways are I-30, I-20 and SH 360, which connects to both. TxDOT built a fully directional interchange at I-30 and SH 360 and rebuilt the Six Flags Drive bridge over I-30, widening it from two lanes to five. From Camp Wisdom and Sublett Roads, south of I-20, SH 360 continues as the 360 Tollway, a 9.7-mile toll road that opened in 2018 and runs through Arlington, Grand Prairie and Mansfield to US 287.

The city's public transportation is Arlington On-Demand, a shared-ride service operated by Via. Riders book through the Arlington Transportation app or by phone, and it runs 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. on Saturdays. It also serves the CentrePort station on the Trinity Railway Express commuter line and the Eastchase Parkway area of Fort Worth.

Arlington's economic development office lists the General Motors assembly plant at about 6,100 employees, GM Financial at 2,600, UT Arlington at 5,200, Texas Health Resources at 4,000, the Texas Rangers at 2,800 and Six Flags Over Texas at 1,800. The same page also names D.R. Horton and Bell Textron among the companies based in Arlington.

School districts

Addresses in Arlington fall into these public school districts:

  • Mansfield ISD
  • Kennedale ISD
  • Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD

District lines do not follow city limits. Confirm the district for a specific address with the district itself before you write an offer.

Arlington homes by the numbers

The median home in Arlington was built in 1986. About 33% of homes predate 1980 and 8% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $304,700, close to the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Arlington is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

Parker County picked this site for its county seat and named it for Jefferson Weatherford. The town incorporated in 1858. The Texas and Pacific Railway arrived in 1880, and the Santa Fe followed in 1887. A local line, the Weatherford, Mineral Wells and Northwestern, began operating in 1891 (Handbook of Texas). The city's FY 2026 adopted budget dates a home-rule charter to 1918 and puts the land area at 28.95 square miles.

That budget places the Weatherford Water Supply Reservoir about five miles northeast of town. It lists Lake Weatherford at 1,158 surface acres, a pool elevation of 896 feet, and a maximum depth of 39 feet. The parks department, in the same document, maintains 860 acres made up of 19 parks, 9 rental facilities, and 25 sports fields. The budget's park names include Holland Lake, Soldier Springs, Cartwright, Cherry, Love Street, McGratton, and Miller. Chandor Gardens accounts for 3.5 developed acres, including a residence of 5,685 square feet, plus 13.5 undeveloped acres next to it.

First Monday grew out of the trade day held when court was in session, when people brought produce and livestock into town. The handbook says it still includes a flea market, produce, and livestock. The same entry describes Weatherford as the shipping and retail point for Parker County farms and ranches once the rail lines were in place (Handbook of Texas).

Getting around and work

Besides U.S. 180, U.S. 80, Interstate 20, and State Highway 171, the handbook names Farm roads 51, 920, 1884, and 2421 (Handbook of Texas). The FY 2026 budget places the city 25 miles west of Fort Worth. It also describes two reinvestment zones drawn around retail corridors: TIRZ No. 1 is a 1,593-acre area inside the I-20 corridor, and TIRZ No. 2 is an 1,835-acre area along Main Street from I-20 to 4th Street and along U.S. 180, called Fort Worth Highway in that section.

Weatherford operates its own electric, water, and wastewater systems. The FY 2026 budget says a municipal utility board oversees those systems, which reach the city and much of the area out toward the reservoir northeast of town. The facts page in that budget also names the Town Creek Hike and Bike Trail and a boardwalk.

The same budget's employer list shows Weatherford ISD with 1,131 employees, Parker County with 495, Weatherford College with 445, the City of Weatherford with 434, Medical City with 402, Wal-Mart with 350, and C.D. Hartnett with 276. The district's community page calls Weatherford ISD the largest employer in Weatherford (Weatherford ISD). The handbook says Weatherford College was already the local college in the 1890s and that the campus expanded in the 1960s (Handbook of Texas).

Weatherford homes by the numbers

The median home in Weatherford was built in 1998. About 32% of homes predate 1980 and 27% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $312,100, close to the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Weatherford is in Parker County. For Parker County addresses, the Parker County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Parker County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

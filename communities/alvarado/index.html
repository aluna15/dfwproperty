Homes and neighborhoods

William Balch staked a claim in the winter of 1849, and in 1853 he and G. H. Sigler laid out half-acre town lots. The handbook says postal service was in place by the summer of 1854. After the Santa Fe and the Missouri, Kansas and Texas arrived, the count passed 1,000.

Later figures in that Handbook entry: an estimated 1,200 residents and more than fifty businesses by the mid-1920s; 1,324 people in 1943; 4,129 in 1988, when about sixty businesses were operating; 2,918 in 1990; 3,288 in 2000; and 3,785 in 2010. The half-acre lots are the original town grid. Newer houses sit out along the freeway and U.S. 67, but this entry does not name individual subdivisions.

City offices for streets, water, and drainage are at 104 West College Street. That public-services page is also where the city posts its I-35W project overview, in English and in Spanish.

Getting around and work

Interstate 35W is the freeway, and U.S. 67 is the crossing highway, fifteen miles east of Cleburne (Handbook of Texas). U.S. 81 is the third road named at that junction. Voters incorporated the town in 1878. In 1881 the Santa Fe and the Katy both reached it. A bank had been chartered in 1880, and by 1890 that entry puts the count past 2,000, with the Alvarado Bulletin, cotton gins, a hotel, and an opera house already open.

For the construction zone on the freeway, use the city's I-35W project sheets. Public Services lists an overview in English, an overview in Spanish, and the virtual public-hearing notices. The department phone on that page is 817-790-3351, and the after-hours line is 817-301-4947. The Spanish sheet sits beside the English one, so you can read the city's own description of the freeway work in either language.

Alvarado homes by the numbers

The median home in Alvarado was built in 1999. About 33% of homes predate 1980 and 34% were built in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $249,000, below the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Alvarado is in Johnson County. For Johnson County addresses, the Johnson County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Johnson County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

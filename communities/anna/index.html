Homes for sale in Anna, Texas

Anna is in northern Collin County, with US 75 (Central Expressway) running north-south through the city. The downtown master plan places Anna about 10 miles north of McKinney and 45 miles northeast of Dallas, on about 15 square miles of city land inside a 61-square-mile planning area, and it records incorporation in 1913 (City of Anna downtown plan).

Homes and neighborhoods

The 2021 parks plan names Natural Springs Park and Slayter Creek Park, and it says recreation sites are concentrated mainly on the east side of US 75 (Anna parks plan). The downtown plan sets a study area of slightly over 1.3 square miles. Earlier downtown maps used Hackberry Lane, South Easton Street, FM 455, and Slayter Creek as edges. SH 5, signed Powell Parkway, runs north-south, and FM 455 runs east-west. Sherley Heritage Park and the Anna Train Depot sit beside the railroad. Johnson Park and Geer Park, on the east side, were still undeveloped in that plan (downtown plan).

The parks plan also describes subdivisions then moving through review. The Woods at Lindsey Place, just west of Anna High School and east of US 75, is a single-family subdivision with a multi-family component and about 22 acres of parkland. Villages of Hurricane Creek, west of Central Expressway, was expected to dedicate about 148 acres. Anna Crossing is a multi-phase single-family subdivision, also with a multi-family piece, and a 28-acre park (Anna parks plan).

A 2025–2026 city work plan says public improvement districts were created for AnaCapri North, Rockhill, and Liberty Hills (Anna work plan). Anna ISD says its area covers more than 64 square miles and includes the city of Anna plus sections of surrounding communities. The district office is at 151 West Rosamond Parkway (Anna ISD).

Getting around and work

Daily travel uses US 75. The downtown plan says Anna began as an 1872 stop on the Houston & Texas Central Railroad, later Southern Pacific, and that a DART railroad right-of-way still runs north-south through downtown, parallel to Powell Parkway. Fourth Street is the historic grid next to that right-of-way. The plan's action list calls for an ongoing dialogue with DART about bringing public transportation to Anna. It also says the downtown stretches of SH 5 and FM 455 are TxDOT roads, drawn as a divided four-lane section in the thoroughfare plan (downtown plan).

The 2025–2026 work plan schedules two additional lanes on Rosamond Parkway from US 75 to Buddy Hayes (Anna work plan). Anna ISD's homepage lists 6,719 students enrolled and 964 employees (Anna ISD). The district's about page says it operates nine campuses: five elementary, two middle, one high school, and an alternative education center (Anna ISD about).

The parks plan describes an Anna Business Center as an employment area the trail network should reach, and a 75 Center along US 75 for commercial and higher-density residential place types. The same plan's growth scenarios put a 2050 population between 42,000 and 84,000, using 3.5 percent and 5.5 percent compound rates. Those figures are planning estimates in the 2021 document (Anna parks plan).

Anna homes by the numbers

Most of Anna's housing is recent. The median home was built in 2012, and 58% of homes went up in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $351,700, above the Texas median of $283,800.

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Anna is in Collin County. For Collin County addresses, the Collin County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Collin County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

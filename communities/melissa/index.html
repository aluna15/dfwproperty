Homes for sale in Melissa, Texas

Melissa is in north-central Collin County, seven miles northeast of McKinney and just off State Highway 75. The city's FY2025 financial report says the city covers about 21 square miles between the East Fork of the Trinity River and Sister Grove Creek, and that Highway 75, State Highway 121 (Sam Rayburn Highway), and Highway 5 (McKinney Street) all cross the city (City of Melissa history, FY2025 financial report).

Homes and neighborhoods

City history says a post office opened on September 9, 1853, at Foster Crossing, about 3 miles north of today's Melissa, and that Melissa incorporated in the early 1970s (City of Melissa history). The housing on the ground now is tracked in school-boundary lists more than in a single old town plat. Melissa ISD's March 2025 elementary-boundary article groups named neighborhoods that include Liberty, North Creek, Villages of Melissa, Trails of Melissa, Willow Wood, Meadow Park, and Hunter Lakes, among others assigned to five elementary campuses (Melissa ISD boundaries).

The FY2025 financial report's capital-asset table lists 126 acres of parks for 2025 and 100 acres for the Melissa Sports Facility. A note on that table ties a 25-acre increase to Melissa Lake Park, which includes the lake, a parking lot, and trails around the lake, and it records a FY2025 purchase of an HOA portion at Lake Perry Fisher (FY2025 financial report). The same report says Melissa sits a short distance from Lake Lavon and Lake Texoma. The 2015 land-use plan recommends concentrated retail along SH 5 and SH 121 and at Melissa Road and US 75, and it treats retail as appropriate in mixed-use areas, the Town Center, and Old Town (2015 land-use plan).

The city FAQ sends school questions to Melissa ISD at the district home office, 1904 Cooper Street (City of Melissa FAQ). City Hall is at 3411 Barker Avenue (City of Melissa FAQ).

Getting around and work

The FY2025 report describes Highway 75 as the road that also meets Interstate 635, and State Highway 121 as the northeast-southwest highway toward DFW International Airport and the Dallas North Tollway. The capital-asset table lists 120.4 miles of streets in 2025, 160.92 miles of water mains, and 125.64 miles of sanitary sewer (FY2025 financial report). The 2015 plan says Old Town grew up beside the railroad in the early 1900s, and it maps a transit-oriented area for an anticipated station location identified by NCTCOG, with a minimum of 25 units per acre (2015 land-use plan).

That same financial report prints a 2025 principal-employer table, sourced to the Texas Workforce Commission. Melissa ISD is listed first, with 1,128 employees. H-E-B follows with 525, Redden Concrete with 400, Beam Concrete Construction with 350, and Buc-ee's with 315. Kroger is listed at 166, the City of Melissa at 137, NTMWD Regional Disposal at 83, and Calhar Utility Construction at 75 (FY2025 financial report).

Melissa ISD's offices remain at 1904 Cooper Street, and the city publishes that phone line as 972-837-2411 (City of Melissa FAQ). The district's boundary article names five elementary campuses: Harry McKillop, Highland, North Creek, Sumeer, and Willow Wood (Melissa ISD boundaries).

Melissa homes by the numbers

Most of Melissa's housing is recent. The median home was built in 2015, and 73% of homes went up in 2010 or later. Owners put the median home value at $454,000, above the Texas median of $283,800.

These are survey averages for 2020–2024, so they trail what homes sell for today. For current prices, look at the live listings above or ask for a comparable-sales report.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates (tables b08013, b08303, b25003, b25018, b25024, b25034, b25035, b25064, b25077).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Melissa is in Collin County. For Collin County addresses, the Collin County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Collin County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

Homes and neighborhoods

Colleyville grew out of farm country. The city's own history says the area was settled in the mid-1800s as five small communities, with the original crossroads at what is now East Glade Road and Bransford Road, and that it later became known for its cantaloupes, watermelons, squash and tomatoes. The name honors Dr. Lilburn Howard Colley, who settled there in the 1880s (City of Colleyville FY26 budget).

Colleyville incorporated in January 1956 with less than two square miles and about 250 residents. The city says its closeness to DFW Airport drove substantial growth in the late 1970s and early 1980s and led to the development of its residential neighborhoods (City of Colleyville). The Handbook of Texas adds that the town grew by about 3,000 residents a decade through the 1970s and 1980s (Handbook of Texas).

Lot size has been a local issue for decades. In the early 1980s, according to the Handbook, residents split between limiting new construction to single-family homes on large lots and allowing smaller lots and multifamily housing (Handbook of Texas). The current city budget says Colleyville means to keep its residential character and country feel while adding retail (City of Colleyville).

For recreation, the Cotton Belt Trail for walkers and cyclists runs through the city (Wikipedia), and the Colleyville Center hosts community events (City of Colleyville).

Getting around and work

State Highway 26 cuts through Colleyville from southwest to northeast, and SH 121 runs along part of its eastern edge (Wikipedia). The city describes itself as near SH 121 and SH 183 and close to DFW Airport (City of Colleyville FY26 budget).

TEXRail trains pass through Colleyville but do not stop there (Wikipedia). The stations on either side of the city are North Richland Hills/Smithfield to the west and Grapevine/Main Street to the east, with service continuing to DFW Airport Terminal B (Trinity Metro).

For work, the Handbook of Texas ties Colleyville's growth to its proximity to Fort Worth, where most residents historically worked (Handbook of Texas). Next door in Grapevine, the city lists DFW International Airport, the Gaylord Texan Resort and Convention Center, Bass Pro Shops and the North American headquarters of Kubota among its major employers (Grapevine Economic Development).

For health care, Colleyville's budget points residents to nearby hospitals including Texas Health Harris Methodist Hospital Southlake (City of Colleyville).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Colleyville is in Tarrant County. For Tarrant County addresses, the Tarrant County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Tarrant County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

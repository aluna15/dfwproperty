Homes and neighborhoods

In 1873, after the Texas and Pacific crossed the county, C. C. Nash and John G. Moore bought 320 acres and laid out streets and lots. The post office opened that year. The town was named for Robert A. Terrell, whose farm lay on the western edge, and it incorporated in 1875. By the mid-1880s about 3,000 people lived here. Terrell State Hospital opened in 1885. In 1892 Edward Howland Robinson Green made Terrell the headquarters of the Texas Midland Railroad. During World War II, British pilots trained at the field that is now the municipal airport. The Handbook's 1990 count was 12,490 and its 2000 count was 13,606. In the 1980s the local economy rested on agricultural businesses and on plants making machine parts, clothing, and plastic goods (Handbook of Texas).

The city profile lists 13,606 residents in 2000, 17,842 in 2017, and 18,567 in 2021, and it names Terrell ISD, plus a Trinity Valley Community College campus, for public instruction from the earliest years through high school. City Hall is at 201 East Nash Street. Elevation is given as 440 feet (City of Terrell).

Kings Creek Park, at 259 Kings Creek Drive, covers 31.5 acres south of Interstate 20 (City of Terrell).

Getting around and work

The municipal airport is owned by the city and sits at the junction of Interstate 20, U.S. Highway 80, and State Highway 34. The field is the site of the No. 1 British Flight Training School, the first U.S. location used to train Royal Air Force pilots during World War II. An industrial park sits next to the runway. Apex Aero Center is at 400 British Flying School Boulevard, and the Major William F. Long Terminal is at 119 Silent Wings Boulevard (City of Terrell).

A city page on local industry says Trinity Valley Community College's Terrell campus is at 1200 East Interstate 20, and the TVCC Health Science Center is at 1551 South State Highway 34. In 2016 Terrell ISD voters approved a bond that renovated the elementary campuses, added a separate academy for the sixth year at the Furlough Middle campus, and built the ExCel Center (City of Terrell). The Texas and Pacific arrived in 1873, and the Texas Midland Railroad put its headquarters here in 1892 (Handbook of Texas).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Terrell is in Kaufman County. For Kaufman County addresses, the Kaufman County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Kaufman County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

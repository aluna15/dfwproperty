Homes and neighborhoods

The Handbook of Texas says T. K. Seago opened a store in 1876 and the place was known as Seago, the post office name changed to Seagoville in 1910, and the town incorporated in 1926. A new high school finished in 1959, and five years later the local district became part of Dallas ISD. The handbook also describes a federal detention station from the Depression years, later the federal correctional institution, as one of the supports of the local economy.

The Seagoville Economic Development Corporation describes the housing as a range from starter homes and established neighborhoods to larger single-family properties. It counts seven parks and open-space areas totaling about 105 acres, with trails, picnic areas, athletic fields, tennis courts, and a fishing pond. Dallas County’s Post Oak Preserve, 334.7 acres, sits inside Seagoville. The same page says Dallas ISD, Mesquite ISD, and Crandall ISD all have attendance areas in the city, with Dallas ISD’s Seagoville feeder pattern covering the largest share. The corporation’s home page also names Santorini as a master-planned community (EDC).

Getting around and work

State Highway 175 and the Southern Pacific line are the old spine of town, and Interstate 635, U.S. Highway 75, and Interstate 20 skirt the community, the Handbook of Texas says. The economic development corporation cites more than 68,000 vehicles a day on U.S. 175 at East Simonds Road. That page lists a 6-acre corner at U.S. 175 and East Simonds Road, a 14.51-acre industrial tract on the U.S. 175 corridor with access toward Interstate 20 and Interstate 635, and a 19-acre tract on West Malloy Bridge Road, with two ponds, near the older downtown.

The correctional institution remains the employer the handbook ties to Seagoville’s economy from the 1930s onward, and that account also says the main office and warehouse of Gibson Discount Stores opened in Seagoville in 1938 (Handbook of Texas). Current attendance zones, rather than a single local district, split among Dallas ISD, Mesquite ISD, and Crandall ISD (city economic development page).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Seagoville is in Dallas County. For Dallas County addresses, the Dallas County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Dallas County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

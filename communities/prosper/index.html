Homes and neighborhoods

Prosper was laid out in 1902 as a stop on the St. Louis, San Francisco and Texas Railway, in the Blackland Prairie farm country called Elm Valley. It incorporated in 1914. The Handbook of Texas says the population fell to 271 by 1940 and counts 2,097 residents in 2000 (Handbook of Texas).

The town's 2024 stormwater report describes 27 square miles in the Northern Blackland Prairie, about 35 miles north of downtown Dallas, with a 2020 census count of 30,174 and a projected build-out of 70,000. Stormwater runs to Lewisville Lake and Lake Lavon by way of Doe Branch, Wilson Creek, Rutherford Creek, and Parvin Branch (Town of Prosper).

A December 2022 development report shows 969 single-family residential permits issued that year. The year-to-date subdivision table in the same report includes Windsong Ranch, Star Trail, Frontier Estates, Hills at Legacy, and Lakes at Legacy (Town of Prosper).

Getting around and work

State Highway 289, Preston Road, is the highway the Handbook of Texas uses to place the town (Handbook of Texas). U.S. 380 forms the southern edge. The town's stormwater report calls Prosper a home-rule municipality with a council-manager government, about 35 miles north of downtown Dallas, and says construction-site inspections increased over the prior year (Town of Prosper). Council minutes from 2013 describe land north of U.S. 380, south of Prosper Trail, west of Preston Road, and east of the Dallas North Tollway (Town of Prosper).

The December 2022 development report also tracks nonresidential cases, including Prosper Fire Station No. 4, a hotel and retail site in Gates of Prosper, and a mixed-use amendment called HUB 380 on 45.6 acres in Hunter Gateway Center (Town of Prosper). The town's schools page describes Prosper ISD and notes Collin College's annual enrollment of about 53,000 credit and continuing-education students (Town of Prosper).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Prosper is in Collin County, with parts in Denton County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Collin County addresses, the Collin County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Collin County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

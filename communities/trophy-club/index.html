Homes and neighborhoods

Trophy Club began as a planned community around a golf course. The Handbook of Texas says Houston developers took the idea to the Westlake council in 1973, Ben Hogan designed an 18-hole course (the only one he designed, according to that history), and the name came from a proposal to exhibit his trophies. Voters incorporated the town on January 19, 1985. The Handbook puts the town at 2,385 acres, with two golf courses and nature trails (Handbook of Texas).

Town history adds that land assembly for the project reached 2,500 acres starting in 1972, and that by 1977 the development had an 18-hole course, a pool, tennis courts, and a clubhouse. In 1992 ClubCorp added nine holes so the town had two full 18-hole courses, later named for Ben Hogan and Kathy Whitworth. The Highlands of Trophy Club followed a public improvement district formed in 2007, which the town says opened the way for about 1,400 new homes (Town of Trophy Club).

Independence Park is a 10-acre park with four baseball fields, tennis courts, and a playground. The town says it opened between 2003 and 2005, along with a community pool (Town of Trophy Club). An election notice says Trophy Club is in two counties, with the majority of homes in Denton County and approximately 150 homes under Tarrant County (Town of Trophy Club).

Getting around and work

State Highway 114 is the highway the Handbook uses to locate the town, about fifteen miles southwest of Denton (Handbook of Texas). Town history says the Texas and Pacific Railroad laid track through the area of today's Roanoke, Trophy Club, and Westlake in 1881 (Town of Trophy Club).

Trophy Club Park, at 2885 Trophy Park Drive, borders the northwest edge of Grapevine Lake. A town newsletter says the park is 877 acres that Trophy Club leases from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (Town of Trophy Club). The town took control of that land in 2002, when it was called Marshall Creek Park, under a 25-year Corps lease, and renamed it Trophy Club Park in 2007 (Town of Trophy Club).

Northwest ISD says it serves Trophy Club, and the town's district page lists Byron Nelson High School, Medlin Middle School, Samuel Beck Elementary, and Lakeview Elementary inside the town (Town of Trophy Club). The district office is not in Trophy Club; Northwest ISD lists its administration building at 2001 Texan Drive in Justin (Northwest ISD).

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Most of Trophy Club is in Denton County, with parts in Tarrant County. Check which county a specific address is in, because the county decides which appraisal district and tax office handle it. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

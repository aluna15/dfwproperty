Homes and neighborhoods

The town was laid out in 1886 as a stop on the Santa Fe Railroad, on land owned by Elizabeth Huling. Cattle from north Denton County had been driven through the site, and wheat was a large part of the early economy. The Handbook of Texas also notes horse farms for registered stock around Sanger by the 1980s.

The parks department maintains 87 acres that include a sports complex, parks, ballfields, and other public land. John Porter Sports Park, at 2201 South Stemmons, has three softball fields. The city has had a second sports park designed for the east side of I-35, across from John Porter (projects update).

On the future land use map adopted 4 April 2022, the municipal-boundary column — the city limits plus some adjacent extraterritorial land — shows 2,348 acres of medium residential, 394 acres of multi-family, and 298 acres of town residential. Ranch and estate residential, 9,540 acres, is in the extraterritorial column. The city says the map shows relationships between uses and is not for scaling exact acreage (future land use map).

Sanger ISD counts 14 subdivisions now building, with more than 200 lots available, plus 19 future subdivisions and more than 12,500 future lots, and ties that pipeline to residential and industrial development. Its campus list places six schools in town, including Sanger High School at 3001 FM 455.

Getting around and work

TxDOT, rather than the city street division, maintains three roads in town: Chapman (Highway 455), Fifth Street (Business I-35), and I-35. County roads are handled by Denton County Road and Bridge (Streets / Traffic).

Sanger began as a railroad shipping point. The Handbook of Texas says the original line later declined, and a state highway started in 1919 reached Sanger from Dallas in 1920. The same entry, revised in 1995, lists manufacturing then in town: bank equipment, plastic molding, paper products, fertilizer spreaders, sheet metal, toilet compartments, and cabinets. Read that list as a historical snapshot, not a current employer roll.

The city treats Sanger ISD as a separate government and a standing partner. That page lists City Hall at 502 Elm Street. The district's growth page says enrollment is expected to more than double by the 2035 school year and pass 6,400 students, after a gain of nearly 400 students since 2019. The parks office is at 201 Bolivar Street.

Sanger homes by the numbers

Property taxes and the homestead exemption

Sanger is in Denton County. For Denton County addresses, the Denton County appraisal district sets the value your taxes are based on, and bills come from the Denton County tax office.

Once you buy a home and live in it, file for the residence homestead exemption with the appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, with an extra $60,000 for owners 65 or older or disabled. The general deadline is before May 1, and if you buy after January 1 you can get the exemption for the part of the year after you qualify (Texas Comptroller).

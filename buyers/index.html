Buying a home in Dallas–Fort Worth

How a Texas home purchase actually runs, from the first showing to the keys, and where buyers lose money by not knowing the steps.

1. Sign a buyer agreement and get pre-approved

In Texas, since January 1, 2026, an agent working with a home buyer must have a written agreement with that buyer before showing any home, or before presenting an offer if no home is shown. The agreement states the agent's services, when it ends, whether you can work with other agents while it lasts, and how much the broker is paid; broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable (TREC). A lender's pre-approval letter comes next: most sellers want one with any offer.

2. Make an offer on the TREC contract

Most resale homes in Texas sell on the Texas Real Estate Commission's One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale), form 20-19. Price is only one term. The others decide how protected you are:

  • Earnest money and option fee. Under paragraph 5 of the TREC 20-19 contract, both go to the escrow agent within 3 days after the effective date.
  • Option period. For the option fee, you get the unrestricted right to terminate within the number of days you negotiate, by 5:00 p.m. local time on the last day (TREC 20-19, paragraph 5). This is when you inspect.
  • Financing. The third-party financing addendum sets how long you have to get final loan approval.
  • Title policy and survey. The contract says who pays for the owner's title policy and whether an existing survey will do.

3. Inspect during the option period

Hire a licensed inspector early in the option period, not late. Read the seller's disclosure too: Texas requires most sellers of a single-unit home to give buyers a written disclosure notice; new homes never lived in, foreclosure and court-ordered sales, and some estate and family transfers are exempt (Texas Property Code §5.008; TREC's version is form 55-1). If the inspection turns up problems, you can negotiate repairs or a price change, or walk away before the option period ends.

4. Appraisal, title and closing

Your lender orders the appraisal. The title company searches the title, issues a commitment and closes the sale. At closing you sign, the loan funds and you get the keys.

5. File your homestead exemption

Once you live in the home, file for the residence homestead exemption with your county appraisal district. School districts must exempt $140,000 of a homestead's value, and the general deadline is before May 1 (Texas Comptroller). Each city page links to the right appraisal district.

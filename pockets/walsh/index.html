Walsh homes in Fort Worth, Texas

The developer places Walsh inside the Fort Worth city limits, in both Tarrant County and Parker County, on Interstate 30 (Walsh). The property covers more than 7,200 acres, and the first 1,700 acres are the part in development (Walsh).

The pocket

The about page says a master concept plan was completed in 2000 and approved by the Fort Worth City Council. At full build-out that plan keeps 2,300 acres of open space and more than 32 miles of hike-and-bike trails (Walsh).

The facts page lists the access as Exit 1A onto Walsh Ranch Parkway (Walsh). Fort Worth created Public Improvement District 16, Walsh Ranch/Quail Valley, on September 27, 2016, by Resolution No. 4686-09-2016. The service and assessment plan describes approximately 1,703.5682 acres within the corporate limits and the extraterritorial jurisdiction, anticipated as seven phases and about 3,317 homes (City of Fort Worth). The city's PID page says three of those seven improvement areas are complete and that the district funds streets, drainage, utilities, and parks (City of Fort Worth).

Homes

House sizes on the facts page run from about 1,500 square feet to more than 5,000 square feet. That page projects a population of nearly 50,000 at build-out and places Walsh in the Aledo Independent School District. Amenities it describes include a 10,000-square-foot Athletic Club and more than 24 miles of walking and biking trails (Walsh).

The homes page says Founders Park, the first neighborhood park, is surrounded by 10 model homes open to the public daily. Builders named on that page include Britton Homes, David Weekley Homes, Drees Custom Homes, Highland Homes, Perry Homes, and Toll Brothers (Walsh).
